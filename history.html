The History of AI - Major Advancements Through Time

Pre-20th Century 1900 to 1950 1951 to 1980 1981 to 2000 2001 to 2020 2021 to Present
Pre-20th Century
1726

Jonathan Swift's novel Gulliver's Travels describes the Engine, a machine that rearranges words on wooden blocks to produce books, an early imagining of what is now algorithmic text generation.

1900 to 1950
1914

Spanish engineer Leonardo Torres y Quevedo shows El Ajedrecista, the first chess-playing machine, which plays a king and rook versus king endgame on its own.

1921

Karel Čapek's play R.U.R. (Rossum's Universal Robots) opens in London and is the first time the word "robot" is used in English.

1939

John Vincent Atanasoff and Clifford Berry build the Atanasoff-Berry Computer, one of the earliest digital electronic computers and one that used binary to represent data.

1943

Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts publish a paper that treats the brain as a computational system and introduces the idea of artificial neural networks.

1950

Alan Turing publishes "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," which proposes the imitation game, now known as the Turing Test.

1951 to 1980
1951

Marvin Minsky and Dean Edmunds build SNARC, the first artificial neural network, which simulated a rat learning to navigate a maze.

1955

The term "artificial intelligence" is first coined in a proposal for the Dartmouth Summer Research Project. The workshop took place in 1956 and is generally considered the birth of the field.

1957

Frank Rosenblatt develops the Perceptron, an early neural network that learns to classify data by adjusting the weights of its inputs.

1958

John McCarthy develops Lisp, which becomes the most popular programming language in AI research.

1959

Arthur Samuel's checkers program improves with experience, and he coins the term "machine learning."

1965

Joseph Weizenbaum develops ELIZA, a program that mimics human conversation in typed natural language.

1966

Shakey, developed at SRI, becomes the first mobile robot able to reason about its own actions.

1973 AI winter

James Lighthill's critical report to the British Science Research Council leads to deep cuts in UK AI funding and contributes to the first AI winter.

1981 to 2000
1982

Japan launches the Fifth Generation Computer Systems Project to build computers capable of logical reasoning and problem-solving.

1984 AI winter

Roger Schank and Marvin Minsky warn of a coming AI winter. Within three years, interest and funding drop, a period known as the second AI winter.

1986

David Rumelhart, Geoffrey Hinton and Ronald Williams publish their paper on backpropagation, which becomes a foundation for modern deep learning.

1989

Yann LeCun's team at AT&T Bell Labs uses backpropagation to train a neural network to read handwritten ZIP codes.

1997

Sepp Hochreiter and Jürgen Schmidhuber introduce Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks.

IBM's Deep Blue defeats world chess champion Garry Kasparov in a six-game match.

2001 to 2020
2007

Fei-Fei Li and her team at Princeton start ImageNet, a huge database of labeled images that drives progress in computer vision.

2009

Rajat Raina, Anand Madhavan and Andrew Ng show that GPUs can far outperform CPUs for deep learning.

2011

IBM Watson defeats Jeopardy! champions Ken Jennings and Brad Rutter.

Apple launches Siri, a voice assistant built into iOS.

2012

AlexNet, from Geoffrey Hinton's team at the University of Toronto, reaches a 16% error rate on the ImageNet challenge, down from the previous year's best of 25%.

2016

Google DeepMind's AlphaGo defeats top Go player Lee Sedol 4 to 1.

2020

OpenAI introduces GPT-3, a language model with 175 billion parameters.

DeepMind's AlphaFold 2 accurately predicts the 3D structures of proteins from their amino acid sequences.

2021 to Present
2021

OpenAI introduces DALL·E, which generates images from text descriptions.

2022

In November, OpenAI launches ChatGPT, built on GPT-3.5, as a free research preview, bringing generative AI to a broad public audience.

2023

In March, OpenAI releases GPT-4, which can accept both text and image inputs.

2024

The European Parliament adopts the EU AI Act, a risk-based framework for regulating AI.

The Nobel Prizes in Physics and Chemistry recognize foundational contributions to AI and machine learning.

2025

AI agents that can plan tasks, use software and complete multistep workflows become a major area of development.

2026

Reports of AI agents taking unintended actions during testing raise new concerns about reliability, security and control.

Source: "The history of AI" by Tim Mucci, Matthew Finio and Amanda Downie, IBM Think (updated 19 August 2026).
